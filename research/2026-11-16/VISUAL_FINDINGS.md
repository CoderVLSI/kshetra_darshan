# Visual layout findings from satellite references

Reviewed from the contact sheets and selected full-size captures on 16 November 2026. These observations identify visible site context and roof massing. They do not establish dimensions, hidden walls or the identity of every internal shrine. Directions such as left/right below refer to the screenshot frame, not ritual directions.

| Destination | Visible layout / context | Modelling implication |
|---|---|---|
| Shankari / Koneswaram | Temple occupies a narrow rocky promontory, with sea on multiple sides and a constrained approach | Build the headland and approach first; a broad inland square compound is inappropriate |
| Kanchi Kamakshi | Large rectangular tank adjoining substantial connected roofed precincts, within dense urban blocks | Preserve tank-to-temple relationship and internal building density |
| Shrinkhala / Pandua | Current Minar and its grounds are visible among town blocks | This documents the surviving monument, not an ancient Shrinkhala floor plan |
| Chamundeshwari | Compact temple precinct on a developed hilltop, with nearby separate temple roofs and roads | Distinguish main enclosure, neighbouring shrines and hillside approach |
| Jogulamba / Alampur | Multiple temple compounds lie beside the river; modern complex and older structures are distinguishable as separate groups | Avoid merging every nearby temple into a single generic courtyard |
| Bhramaramba / Srisailam | A substantial enclosed complex with gateway structures, multiple roofed internal masses and surrounding facilities | Retain the shared Shiva-Shakti campus and connected circulation |
| Kolhapur Ambabai | Dense connected temple roofscape within the city, not an isolated star platform | Trace actual connected halls and shrine masses before architectural detail |
| Mahur Renuka | Long ridge approach and a sequence of structures amid steep wooded slopes | Terrain, paths and levels are fundamental to recognition |
| Harsiddhi / Ujjain | Compact shrine precinct within a wider city, close to large open water and other temples | Keep Harsiddhi distinct from the lakefront and other Ujjain temple campuses |
| Pithapuram / Padagaya | Multiple temple structures and a substantial pool in a shared urban campus | Represent the shared precinct rather than a lone Puruhutika shrine |
| Biraja / Jajpur | Enclosed roofed temple ensemble with smaller structures and surrounding open grounds | Match subsidiary placement to the trust inventory; satellite does not name every roof |
| Draksharamam | A central enclosed temple mass amid town streets, with a separate large tank nearby | Separate tank, enclosures and upper-sanctum structure; do not borrow Samalkota's layout |
| Kamakhya | A connected temple mass in dense hilltop development, with vegetation and approach buildings | Avoid a sparse landscape with an isolated dome |
| Alopi / Prayagraj | Compact urban site reached through streets, not an isolated riverfront compound | Keep the cradle-centred shrine and town setting; Sangam is a different location |
| Jwalamukhi | Initial map lookup selected the town; corrected temple pin is separately recorded | Use the corrected close view, not the initial town-centre image |
| Mangala Gauri / Gaya | Small shrine group on a hill amid dense development and curving approaches | Compact scale, slope and approach matter more than an invented tall central tower |
| Vishalakshi / Kashi | Extremely dense urban fabric near other large temple precincts and the river | The neighbouring Vishwanath campus must not be mistaken for Vishalakshi's footprint |
| Sharada / Kashmir | Ruin site within a river-valley settlement; masonry and setting require close inspection | Use surviving ruins as baseline and keep hypothetical historical restoration separate |
| Madurai Meenakshi | Dense rectangular nested precincts, extensive continuous roofs, multiple towers and an internal tank | Four detached towers and a small central shrine do not reproduce this plan |
| Kalighat | Dense city precinct beside urban streets and watercourse context | Preserve compact urban scale and verify current redevelopment state |
| Tuljapur | Irregular developed precinct constrained by terrain and town, with several approach routes | Do not replace it with a level symmetric hill-fort rectangle |

Read the full-resolution screenshots through [MAPS.md](MAPS.md). Google Maps names and pins are user/provider data, not temple-authority surveys. One Sharada pin includes a user-supplied political suffix; that wording is not adopted as an administrative or historical assertion.

No final Gemini output has been judged here. The earlier game screenshots and model inspection are a pre-completion snapshot; repeat comparisons against Gemini's final assets.
